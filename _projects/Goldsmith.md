Custom jewelry is expensive mostly because of the middlemen. Jewelers hold the connections to manufacturers and designers, and they mark prices up 200% to 500% to cover that, plus the store and the hours of consulting that custom work takes. Buyers can't tell whether the price is fair, so many don't trust the process, and custom pieces end up out of reach for anyone who isn't already wealthy. I described Goldsmith as the Warby Parker of custom jewelry. For the full story, read the [Goldsmith case study]({{ '/projects/Goldsmith%20Case%20Study/' | relative_url }}).

## What I built

Goldsmith has two parts, a design tool and a marketplace.

**A design tool.** A buyer describes a piece in plain language and the platform generates candidate 3D designs using generative CAD on top of open source design software. I chose open source so one person could move quickly, and I planned to train the models on non IP data, with the option for a buyer to protect any design they create. Jewelers and designers get the full version of the tool with cloud GPUs, so a small shop doesn't need expensive hardware to compete.

**A marketplace.** Once a buyer picks a design, it goes to manufacturers, who quote on it through a request for quotation. Manufacturers share business documentation before they can bid, and certifications such as the Responsible Jewellery Council's are shown where a company holds one. Stones come with lab grading reports, for example from GIA, where they are eligible. Buyers get 3D printed models of their top candidates to try on, choose a final design, and expect the finished piece in about three weeks.

## Who pays

The plan had five revenue streams:

- Software licences from $5,000 a year for on premise use and cloud subscriptions from $8,000 a year
- Support contracts with design and technical support engineers for business customers
- Monthly platform fees for jewelers, manufacturers and power users
- Sponsored listings for manufacturers when buyers compare quotes
- A transaction fee on marketplace orders

Consumers come in through the website and social media. Jewelers and manufacturers are reached through LinkedIn, trade expos and account managers, with HubSpot as the CRM.

## How I tested the idea

I wrote the customer discovery interview guide. It starts from a stated problem and value hypothesis, then asks buyers ten questions: how often they buy, what hurts most about custom jewelry, how much they trust a jeweler's price on a 1 to 10 scale, whether they'd design with natural language, whether they'd choose a piece from a 3D preview, and how long they'd wait. I also mapped the existing tools (MatrixGold, RhinoGold, JewelCAD Pro and Blender) and found that none of them combined AI design with a marketplace.

I built the financial model behind the pitch. At the investor pitch in December 2024 I presented a plan, not results: a beta launch with 1,000 users and 20 manufacturers in year one and about $500K in revenue, growing to 10,000 users and $5M+ at 90% retention by year three. Those were targets.

## What I'd test next

The assumption I'd go after first is that buyers will pick a final design from a 3D preview and wait three weeks for it. If they won't, the cost advantage is gone. I'd start with a small run with a few manufacturers and a few dozen real buyers, before building more of the platform.
