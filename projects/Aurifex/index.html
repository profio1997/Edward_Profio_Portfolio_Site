Aurifex Jewelry

Founder of an AI jewelry-design platform and marketplace that connects buyers with verified manufacturers.

Role Founder and CEOWhen 2024 to 2025Team Four founders, built in Carnegie Mellon's Lean Entrepreneurship course

Custom jewelry is expensive mostly because of who you have to go through to get it. Jewelers hold the connections to manufacturers and designers, and they mark prices up 200% to 500% to cover that, plus the cost of a store and the hours of consulting that custom work takes. Buyers can’t judge whether they’re getting a fair price, so many don’t trust the process, and custom jewelry stays out of reach for anyone who isn’t already wealthy. We described Aurifex as the Warby Parker of custom jewelry.

What we built

Aurifex has two halves that feed each other.

A design tool. A buyer describes a piece in plain language and the platform generates candidate 3D designs using generative CAD on top of open-source design software. We chose open source so a small team could move quickly, and we planned to train the models on non-IP data, with the option for a buyer to protect any design they create. Jewelers and designers get the full version of the tool with cloud GPUs, so a small shop doesn’t need expensive hardware to compete.

A marketplace. Once a buyer picks a design, we send it to verified manufacturers, who quote on it through a request for quotation. Manufacturers have to document certifications to international standards such as GIA before they can bid. Buyers get 3D-printed models of their top candidates to try on, choose a final design, and expect the finished piece in about three weeks.

Who pays

We designed five revenue streams so we weren’t dependent on one buyer type:

  • Software licences from $5,000 a year for on-premise use and cloud subscriptions from $8,000 a year
  • Support contracts with design and technical support engineers for business customers
  • Monthly platform fees for jewelers, manufacturers and power users
  • Sponsored listings for manufacturers when buyers compare quotes
  • A transaction fee on marketplace orders

Consumers come in through the website and social media. Jewelers and manufacturers are reached through LinkedIn, trade expos and account managers, with HubSpot as the CRM.

How we tested the idea

I wrote the customer-discovery interview guide with the team. It starts from a stated problem and value hypothesis, then asks buyers ten questions: how often they buy, what hurts most about custom jewelry, how much they trust a jeweler’s price on a 1 to 10 scale, whether they’d design with natural language, whether they’d choose a piece from a 3D preview, and how long they’d wait. We also mapped the competition (MatrixGold, Rhino 3D, JewelCAD Pro and Blender) and found that none of them combines AI design, a marketplace and a cloud platform.

I built the financial model behind the pitch. At the investor pitch in December 2024 we presented a plan, not results: a beta launch with 1,000 users and 20 manufacturers in year one and about $500K in revenue, growing to 10,000 users and $5M+ at 90% retention by year three. Those are targets, and I’d treat them that way.

What I’d test next

The assumption I’d go after first is that buyers will pick a final design from a 3D preview and wait three weeks for it. If they won’t, the whole cost advantage disappears. The first step would be a small run with a few manufacturers and a few dozen real buyers before building more of the platform.
